# SME software services: evidence and decisions

Research checked on 30 October 2026. This is a basis for a first offer, not a
forecast of sales.

## Observed signals

- [Statistics Poland, Information society in Poland in 2026](https://publikacje.new.stat.gov.pl/sites/default/files/2026-10/spoleczenstwo_informacyjne_w_polsce_w_2026.pdf),
  published 21 October 2026: 17.8% of surveyed enterprises use AI, compared with 8.7%
  in 2025. Marketing/sales uses account for 11.1%, office work/management for 9.6%.
  70.7% have their own website. This enterprise survey is not a census of all
  microbusinesses; these percentages do not describe every potential customer.
- [OECD D4SME 2026: Empowering SMEs in the age of AI](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/empowering-smes-in-the-age-of-ai_bf5a9816-en.html),
  published 13 April 2026: more than 2,000 SMEs in 12 countries, explicitly a
  non-representative sample. Ready-made tools are common among respondents, but
  integration into daily operations is uneven. Time, skills and maintenance costs
  remain obstacles. Treat this as directional evidence, not a Polish adoption rate.
- [OECD: Generative AI and the SME Workforce](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/generative-ai-and-the-sme-workforce_2d08b99d-en.html),
  published 5 November 2025, survey conducted in 2024: representative samples of
  more than 5,000 SMEs across seven countries, excluding Poland. 65% of respondents
  using generative AI report better employee performance. This is a self-reported
  result, not a savings guarantee for a new implementation.
- [Eurostat: business software adoption](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20260520-1),
  published 20 May 2026, data for 2025: ERP is used by 41% of small enterprises and
  89% of large enterprises; BI by 11% and 69% respectively. Small means 10-49
  employees/self-employed persons. Selected sectors and enterprises with at least
  10 persons are covered. Adoption gaps do not by themselves prove buying intent.

## Competitive pressure: inference, not a saturation measure

[Wix](https://www.wix.com/ai-website-builder) offers AI-assisted website creation.
[Zapier](https://zapier.com/apps) offers ready-made integrations and automation.
These are available alternatives to some implementation work. Their existence
supports competing on fit, connected workflows and responsibility for the result.
It does not prove that website or chatbot services no longer sell.

Do not lead with generic chatbot installation, AI text/image generation, an agent
without a defined task, or technology names alone. Do not build a custom system
when configuration of an existing product meets the actual need.

## Offer decision

Use three complementary pillars: AI/automation; integrations/reporting; websites,
applications and custom systems. Show tasks a business owner recognizes, such as
handling enquiries, extracting document data, consolidating a sales report or
letting a customer check an order status. These are proposed use cases, not cases
from past customers.

Start an enquiry with a process, existing tools and a desired result. Scope one
useful implementation, evaluate its running cost and support, and expand after
verification. Avoid invented fixed prices, delivery times and ROI.

## What remains to learn

This research does not measure Google keyword volume, LLM prompt frequency, the
number of competing service providers or willingness to pay. No such counts are
claimed. Actual enquiries and owner interviews should determine which task and
customer group deserve a dedicated landing page next. Record the source, requested
outcome, existing tools and whether the enquiry leads to a scoped project. Do not
publish an industry specialism before there is evidence and public proof.
